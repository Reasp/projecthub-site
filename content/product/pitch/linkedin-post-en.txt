Companies now pay for AI coding agents at scale. Almost none of them can control those agents.

That gap is what I'm building ProjectHub for.

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗻𝗼𝘄
→ Enterprise spend on AI coding went from ~$550M in 2024 to $4.0B in 2025 (Menlo Ventures).
→ Gartner renamed its Magic Quadrant to "Enterprise AI Coding Agents" and expects governance and validation to move into dedicated platforms.
→ 97% of enterprise teams use AI coding tools; only 30% have full governance over them (Black Duck, 2026).
→ Cost is the new pain: Uber reportedly capped AI tools at $1,500 per engineer per month after burning its annual budget in four months.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗴𝗮𝗽
GitHub, OpenAI, Google and Cursor orchestrate their own agents. Independent orchestrators are free and stop at the UI. Nobody owns the neutral layer a company needs across every agent it runs: policies, audit, budgets, and proof that the work is actually done.

𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗛𝘂𝗯 𝗶𝘀
A vendor-neutral control room for AI coding agents. Any agent, any model (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, local models). Risky steps wait for a human. Acceptance criteria are checked by the system, not by the agent that wrote the code. Cost is tracked per task. Everything runs inside the customer's perimeter.

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗶𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝘄𝗶𝗻
• Neutrality is the moat. A company running three vendors won't let one of them govern the other two.
• Control is where the money is. Orchestration UIs are free; governance, audit and budgets are what teams pay for.
• Open core: a free MIT client as the funnel, Team at $25–40 per seat (Copilot Enterprise is $39, Cursor Teams $40), Enterprise as an annual on-prem license.

𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗮𝗰𝗵 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀
ProjectHub was built by AI agents using its own methodology: 7 weeks from the first commit, ~123K lines of TypeScript, 168 tasks closed, 76 architecture decisions, 213 test files.

Honest status: v0.1.0, pre-revenue, no external users yet.

𝗡𝗲𝘅𝘁 𝟭𝟮 𝗺𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗵𝘀
1️⃣ Design partners: 5–10 teams, measuring cost per task, cycle time and acceptance rate before and after.
2️⃣ Team edition: several people work in one project on one shared machine, each in their own branch with their own agents. Finished work merges through a queue that checks it against the latest main and requires review by someone other than the author.
3️⃣ Enterprise: on-prem and air-gapped deployments for regulated industries.
